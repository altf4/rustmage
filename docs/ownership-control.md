# Casting cards another player owns

Issue #265 separates immutable physical ownership from spell and permanent control. This first delivery adds a complete Korvold and the Noble Thief body: chapters I and II each create a Treasure, and chapter III targets an opponent, exiles the top three available cards of that player's library and gives the Saga's controller permission to play those exact incarnations this turn. It does not transfer ownership. Complete control-changing card bodies and their duration/layer rules remain gated.

Each owned object and public object view carries both `owner` and `controller`. The controller field describes spell or permanent control; in other zones it returns the owner fallback used by controller queries. It does not grant permission to act on that card. During casting, the explicit acting player supplies cost-reduction, convoke and additional-cost context even before a spell enters the stack. Hand and graveyard actions retain their real owner restrictions. Battlefield mana, activation, sacrifice, targeting, combat, statics, ETB/death/cast observers, APNAP and legend choices use controller. Returning physical cards to hands, graveyards or libraries and identifying a single graveyard continue to use owner.

Stack entry captures the caster. Battlefield entry carries that controller independently of the departing object's last-known facts. Frozen trigger/damage/recipient packets retain the relevant controller after the physical source changes zones; resetting a graveyard object's controller query to its owner cannot redirect an already-bound ability. Entry life/Aura/counter choices follow the incoming controller while ownership remains available for visibility and physical routing. Observation schema82 exposes this distinction, composing the merged schema81 Adventure land-allowance delivery.

Exile permissions bind a grantee, exact object generation and deadline. Independent grants are checked independently rather than accepting only one arbitrary sorted entry for a card. Permission does not waive timing, costs, targets or land allowances. Generic play permission offers normal and alternate faces when otherwise legal. An Adventure's own successful-resolution permission is normal-only, belongs to the resolving controller, and lasts while that new exile incarnation remains there. Omen completion shuffles the physical card into its owner's library; its effects still use the caster's targets, library, choices and resources. Countered or fizzled insets receive no Adventure permission or Omen-resolution shuffle.

The ownership audit intentionally retains owner-based hand/library/graveyard visibility, drawing and milling, graveyard recovery/evidence, grouped graveyard identities, owner-hand/library returns, Omen shuffling and token recycling. Existing Plot/Warp alternative origins remain their actual restricted origins; general foreign exile rights do not grant an unrelated alternative method. More permissive theft bodies involving face-down exile, flexible mana spending, life alternative costs, source-linked duration or control changes remain unsupported until their complete bodies are implemented.

Funding canonicalization retains both physical ownership and control. Sacrificing two otherwise identical controlled mana sources can contribute different evidence to the caster's graveyard. An independent exhaustive physical-state oracle checks 768 mixed-ownership funding/evidence cases, including both players, existing graveyard cards and reversed resource order, without the solver's canonical keys or optimistic bounds.
