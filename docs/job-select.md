## Entry, attachments, and types

Job Select is a genuine suppressible Equipment entry trigger. It creates the Hero before attaching the exact surviving Equipment to that new object. Entry observers therefore see the original 1/1 Hero and its entry-time characteristics; attachment changes apply afterward, before the next priority window. If the Equipment has departed, the Hero still appears. A later incarnation cannot inherit the old attachment. Creation and attachment add no equip payment, target, ward, or activation event. This ordering follows CR 702.182 and the [official FINAL FANTASY release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/final-fantasy-release-notes).

`Card::has_subtype` remains an intrinsic identity query in every zone. `ObjectView::has_subtype` and `DamageFacts::has_subtype` combine that identity with a captured `granted_subtypes` set. Its public serialization is a list of the additional subtype names, rather than a replacement type line. The live engine derives membership from its small attachment index and exact generation links. Multiple grants union naturally; detachment, source departure, recipient departure, and reentry remove the old live grant. Simultaneous departures capture every object's pre-event facts before moving any object.

Battlefield targeting, non-targeted sets, counters, tribe filters, sacrifices, entry/death/damage observers, conditional grants, and activation guards use current or appropriately captured membership. Hidden-zone searches and card filters keep intrinsic characteristics. Speculative funding borrows the same attachment test over its virtual object overlay. Sacrificing Equipment there removes its subtype and keyword effects before later guards or reserved sacrifice costs are checked. Queries mutate no real state.

Dragoon's flying condition and Machinist's artifact count use the Equipment controller. Dark Knight's equip really pays three life, targets normally, requires sorcery timing, and consumes its once-per-turn limit. Other equip costs remain ordinary paid, targeted stack abilities; their flavor labels add no rule.

## Granted abilities and private continuations

A creature's granted ability has that exact creature as its source; the creature's controller controls the ability. The immutable Equipment definition supplies only its program. Cast/draw/damage source indexes expand live attachment recipients; no mutable trait graph is copied into every object. Provenance is captured by value during candidate collection and boxed only when a trigger actually qualifies.

`SpellView.attachment_trigger` now identifies `grantor`, `granting_card`, `recipient`, optional `defender`, and `granted`. `defender` is present only for an attack-declaration event. This generalizes the former attack-only `attacker` field. Existing Equipment-owned attack abilities retain their Equipment source. Granted packets survive detachment or grantor departure; source-relative counters and boosts still require their original creature incarnation.

Planisphere observes successful draws and the actual recipient controller's third draw each turn, including draws before attachment. Its noncreature-cast ability and third-draw ability are independent. Rod and Rapier observe completed casts, including casts later countered. Rod's damage uses the creature's real source facts. Sage targets a currently attacking creature and rechecks that predicate at resolution. Staff gains life for the recipient controller. Thief observes actual combat damage to a player. Each independent Equipment grants its own ability.

Paladin grants real ward 1 to the recipient. Each qualifying trigger retains the exact targeted stack item and its controller as payer, even after detachment. Targeting remains legal when that payer cannot or will not pay. Multiple grants are independent; merely creating or attaching a Hero does not trigger ward.

Ninja draws, then uses the ordinary owner-private mandatory discard choice. After the selected card is genuinely discarded, the damaged player loses its off-stack mana value in life. This is life loss, not source damage, so it cannot create lifelink or damage events. A discarded card's X is zero, as specified in the [official release notes](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/feature/final-fantasy-release-notes). Empty-hand completion loses zero life. A failed final choice rolls back that action while preserving the draw completed earlier.

Summoner uses an owner-private `effect_choice` with `kind: "HandCreature"`. `PutHandCreature { object }` chooses an exact current creature card; `DeclineHandEntry` declines the optional effect. Selected cards stay in Hand until entry is ready. Ordinary creatures enter through the shared replacement/entry pipeline and remain nonattacking. Enchantment creatures must enter tapped and attacking. If opposing planeswalkers exist, `defender_choice` becomes true and `ChooseHandDefender { defender }` chooses an exact current player or planeswalker. The private frame is hidden from the other viewer. The choice is non-targeted and may differ from the original attacker's defender. Current admitted games have no battle permanents. Entering attacking creates no attack-declaration trigger; see [CR 508.4](https://media.wizards.com/2026/downloads/MagicCompRules%2020260925.pdf).
