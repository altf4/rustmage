# Adventure spell faces and owned exile permissions

Twenty-seven complete Adventure pairs are executable in the current candidate, alongside all thirteen Omen pairs, the reviewed Saga lifecycle and typed attachment/Ward bodies: 1,825/5,164 Standard identities, 3,339 gated, 87 token recipes, one emblem, 27 internal Adventure definitions, thirteen Omen definitions and observation schema 81. The full migration remains incomplete. See the [eight-pair delivery record](adventure-land-targets.md) for current candidate evidence and pending gates; the [earlier composition record](review-pr-258.md) retains its scoped historical validation.

Each Adventure remains one physical Card/ObjectRef in every zone. Static internal definitions supply the chosen inset's printed name, cost, mana value, colors, type/subtypes, targets and program. These definitions cannot appear as independent deck cards, including Freeform decks. Ordinary IDs remain stable; internal helper/token numeric positions are scoped to the catalogue fingerprint. Original whole-pair identities and normal printed-name aliases identify the same physical parent.

`Action::AnnounceAdventure { card }` selects the inset before timing, target and payment checks. `CastInfo.face` (`Normal` or `Adventure`) is separate from `CastInfo.method` (`Normal`, `Flashback`, `Plot` or `Warp`). Inset casts retain their physical parent in `CastInfo.physical_card`; the stack's source definition describes the chosen spell. Normal casting metadata has face Normal and no separate physical-card override. Unsupported intrinsic cost-selector/inset combinations remain gated as complete pairs.

Stack predicates, cast observers, reductions, locked costs, live damage facts and captured source LKI use the selected face. Outside a spell context, the physical parent retains its normal characteristics. `Observation.spell_characteristics` exposes the public chosen spell's exact object, physical identity, face, name, cost, mana value, type and colors during announcement, on the stack and during resolution. These views borrow static metadata rather than enlarging every physical object.

Successful inset resolution moves that exact object to face-up exile and creates an owned `AdventurePermission { object, card, player }`. Countering or all-target-illegal fizzle creates no permission. The entitled player uses `AnnounceExile` and pays the normal face's ordinary cost and timing restrictions. Midgar uses a real land play, including the turn's land allowance and tapped entry. The permission is indefinite, restricted to normal play, and removed when that exact exile incarnation leaves. Generic external exile permissions can independently allow an inset; an Adventure permission does not. Current admitted routes cast and later play the caster’s own cards. The shared action emitter still requires the object owner to be the actor: recording a permission for an opponent-owned card does not yet make its normal face playable. Ownership/control changes and opponent-owned card play remain gated full-goal requirements; the current own-card cases do not prove that future route. Clone, restore and dropped-parent branches own their continuation and permission state.
